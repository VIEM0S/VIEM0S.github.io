Starting point

The problem

Running ABC STUDY and building CultureCase GS showed me what local retailers are missing: existing software is designed for other realities. Three constraints shaped the whole design.

  1. 01

    The internet goes down

    A cashier should never keep a customer waiting because the connection dropped. The till has to keep selling offline.

  2. 02

    Customers buy on credit

    Selling on credit is common. You need to track what each customer owes, set limits, and control repayments and write-offs.

  3. 03

    Accounting rules

    Invoices must be numbered without gaps, every year, as SYSCOHADA (the West African accounting standard) requires. And businesses grow: several stores, several employees.

How it's built

Technical choices

Each decision answers one of the real-world constraints. Here are the main ones, and why I made them.

  1. 01

    One database, sealed data

    All businesses share one PostgreSQL database, and every row carries its company's ID. Isolation is enforced by the database itself (Row Level Security), based on the signed-in user's identity rather than what the browser sends: there is no way to see another business's data, even by tampering with a request.

  2. 02

    A till that sells offline

    Without a connection, sales are queued on the device, each with a unique ID to prevent duplicates. When the network returns, they are synced and re-checked by the server (price, cost, actual stock). The cashier is never blocked, and nothing is accepted unchecked.

  3. 03

    Sensitive logic on the server

    Till, credit, sales, and creating or deleting users go through server routes and SQL functions, not the browser. Risky operations require dual approval: a credit limit, a large expense or removing an employee must be approved by someone else, never by the person who requested it.

  4. 04

    SYSCOHADA-compliant invoices

    Each invoice gets a number like FAC-2026-000001, generated by a PostgreSQL sequence specific to each business and each year. Numbering is continuous and gap-free, as the OHADA accounting framework requires.

  5. 05

    Plans for every size

    Each plan's limits (users, stores, products) are defined in a single place in the code and enforced everywhere. A Solo plan at 8,000 FCFA was added for one-person sellers held back by the price of the other plans.
